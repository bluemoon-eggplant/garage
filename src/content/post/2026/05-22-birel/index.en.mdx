After the rotor refused to come off last time, I reluctantly bought the dedicated "rotor puller" tool.
But the crank kept spinning, making it impossible to turn the puller.
So I used the bearing puller I'd bought earlier to hold the rotor puller in place.
And it came right off.

<Image {...IMAGE_SIZES.FIXED.MDX_LG} src={Img4} alt="" />

Next, I removed the case bolts and gently tapped a screwdriver into the bolt holes — the crankcase split open.
As expected, there's some rust visible around the bearings.

<Image {...IMAGE_SIZES.FIXED.MDX_LG} src={Img1} alt="" />

The oil seals look concerning.

<Image {...IMAGE_SIZES.FIXED.MDX_LG} src={Img2} alt="" />

The bearings were also worrying, so I decided to replace them.
Trying to pull them with the external bearing puller, but the housing is so small and light that it's hard to apply force.
After smacking the handle with a rubber mallet, they finally came out.
I really need to buy a heat gun for press-fitting...

<Image {...IMAGE_SIZES.FIXED.MDX_LG} src={Img5} alt="" />

With the oil seals and bearings removed, disassembly is complete. (I don't have the skills or tools to split the crankshaft itself.)
