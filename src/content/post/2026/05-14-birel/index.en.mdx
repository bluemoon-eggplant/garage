## Stripped Down

Everything removable has been removed — the kart is now a bare frame.
There aren't many high-torque fasteners on a kart, so the removal itself wasn't too difficult.
I was also glad there were zero Phillips screws — my least favorite.

<Image {...IMAGE_SIZES.FIXED.MDX_LG} src={Img1} alt="" />

## Engine Disassembly Begins

With no idea of the engine's condition, I wanted to crack it open.
Removed the head and cylinder — the piston and cylinder wall condition looks perfectly fine.
The piston rings still have tension, and the crosshatch pattern is clearly visible.
The crankshaft visible from above is gleaming — that really boosted my motivation. (If this had been bad, the whole project might have been shelved.)

<Image {...IMAGE_SIZES.FIXED.MDX_LG} src={Img2} alt="" />

## Impressions

Working on a 2-stroke engine really makes you appreciate how the head has nothing on it (just a spark plug).
It makes you realize how complex a 4-stroke engine is with its valves, cams, and everything else.
